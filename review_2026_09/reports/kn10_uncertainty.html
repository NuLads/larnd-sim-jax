KN10 uncertainty findings

This report covers the production changes adopted on 6 November and the uncertainty work that followed them. That work asked why the Hessian-based uncertainty failed, then tested a width built from derivatives on production fits. Every number comes from simulation, and errors are in % of the true value. The parameters are Ab (Birks), eField (drift field), tran_diff and long_diff (diffusion) and lifetime (electron lifetime).

Conclusions

  1. Production now runs fit → FR → H → POLISH with a shared compile cache and targets on /sdf/data. The acceptance run on an unseen sample passed all five checks.
  2. The uncertainty to quote stays the ensemble spread of the polished estimate over 30 production fits. That is 0.16 % on Ab, 0.043 % on eField, 1.3 % on tran_diff, 5.2 % on long_diff and 3.7 % on lifetime.
  3. The textbook Hessian is about 3× too narrow (20× on eField) because it treats the fitted dE/dx and track geometry as known. Evaluated at the truth with every nuisance true, the same formula is right.
  4. Most of the gap closes when the curvature is profiled over every fitted nuisance using first derivatives only (Gauss–Newton). Each batch must be bounded so it cannot remove more information than it holds, and with that bound the width is stable on 12 of 12 fits.
  5. On production fits, that width plus the measured optimiser scatter matches the spread for Ab and lifetime. It falls short for tran_diff (1.3×), long_diff (1.5×) and eField (4.4×). The 1 mm endpoint smear shifts eField coherently within each sample, and no derivative at the fit point can see that.

Why the Hessian width failed

Each row below compares the spread of the polished estimate over 8 fits with a predicted width. A ratio of 1 means the width is right, and the shaded band is the range consistent with 1 for 8 fits. These fits used true track endpoints, so the only error sources are the data and the fit itself.

  • The theory holds at the truth. With every nuisance (dE/dx and geometry) set to its true value, the sandwich width falls within the band for four parameters. eField's 1.96 lies above it.
  • Holding the fitted nuisances fixed is the cause. At the fit point, the old Hessian ignores that dE/dx and track positions move together with the calibration. That makes the width 3× too narrow, and 20× on eField.
  • Each nuisance block carries different parameters. Profiling over dE/dx brings Ab and lifetime to 1.1–1.3, and profiling over geometry takes eField from 20 to 1.9. Doing both brings Ab, long_diff and lifetime within about 30 % of 1.

How the profiled width is built

For each batch, one forward and one reverse derivative through the simulator are taken per column. The columns are the 5 calibration parameters, 5 dE/dx probes and every geometry spline coefficient, about 500 nuisances per batch. The curvature is the loss's own Gauss–Newton matrix, so no second derivative of the non-smooth geometry is needed. The bread is B = Σb (Fcc − Fcn Ann−1 Fnc), and the meat comes from the per-batch calibration gradients. The width is the square root of the diagonal of B−1 J B−1. The block costs about 90 s per batch on an A100.

Two variants did not work:

  • The textbook expected information. It ignores the ±2-tick hit-matching window and so counts about twice the information. That makes it 1.4–5× too narrow.
  • A one-step estimate using the profiled score. With dE/dx profiled exactly by finite differences, the curvature was stable, but the profiled score is heavy-tailed. The resulting estimate was 4–8× noisier than the plain conditional estimate on Ab, long_diff and lifetime.

Making the width stable

The loss curvature is not positive definite in the nuisances: it has a median of 2 negative directions per batch, because the hit-matching term is concave. In about 1 batch in 100, an eigenvalue near zero makes that batch's profile term explode. The fix is a per-batch bound, 0 ≤ FcnAnn−1Fnc ≤ Fcc. In words, profiling cannot remove more information than the batch holds. I chose the bound after the first test, so it was checked again on four production fits it had not seen.

The check on production fits

The test used the bounded width on four production end points, EXP29–32 C75H at 20000 with realistic endpoints. It was compared with the spread over the 30 production fits. A derivative cannot see optimiser scatter, which is the run-to-run spread on identical data, so that scatter was added in quadrature. It was measured from four repeated H stages per sample. The rules were written before submission, and every consistency check passed.

eField's spread from the smear behaves like a separate offset in each sample. Its per-fit pulls are +2.5, +1.0, +0.4 and +4.0. Comparing batches within one fit therefore cannot reveal it. The shortfall on tran_diff and long_diff remains under a robust spread measure, so outliers do not cause it. The unbounded variant looks closer on average (0.78 / 2.21 / 1.15 / 0.90 / 0.72), but on two of the four fits it changes by up to 8× with the ridge, so that agreement is an artefact.

What this means

  • Quote the ensemble. The pooled spread of the polished estimate stays the uncertainty for all five parameters.
  • A per-fit width is possible for Ab and lifetime. It needs the bounded Gauss–Newton profile, at about 2.5–3.5 A100-hours per fit, plus a few H-stage reruns for the optimiser scatter, which is 30–40 % of their variance. This becomes useful when real data no longer match the simulated ensemble.
  • eField's uncertainty comes from the endpoint estimate. Only an ensemble with realistic endpoints captures it. An offline comparison of true-end and smeared-end fits of the same data (samples 1–12, no GPU) could show what sets each sample's offset.
  • long_diff remains the weakest parameter, at 5.2 % spread and a +3 % offset. The open levers are coarser dE/dx segments and marginalising over dE/dx, and neither is built yet.
